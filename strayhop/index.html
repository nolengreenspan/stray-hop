    What's what

    On the map

    Red dot — you Your current position. Moves as you walk.
    ★ Yellow star — go get this bike The stray at the top of the page. The closest one to you on foot.
    1 Yellow numbered circle — chosen drop hub The hub you have selected. Tap a different numbered circle to switch.
    3 Blue numbered circles — other drop options Numbers match the ranked list below the map. 1 is the best chain, not the nearest hub.
    ▸ Pale circle with an arrow — your next stray The bike you would walk to after dropping at the chosen hub. Changes when you pick a different hub.
    Small hollow dots — other strays Everything else out there right now. Tap one to see its name and distance.

    The lines

    Black dashed — you walk this From where you are to the bike you are going to get. Straight line, not the real streets.
    Solid blue — you ride this From the bike to the hub you picked. The easy part.
    Faint dashed — your next walk From the drop hub to the next stray. This is the distance the hub list is sorted by.

    The numbers

    360m Walk to it Straight-line distance to the bike. Real streets will be a bit further.
    4h Sitting How long this page has known about the bike. Always a floor — it may have been there far longer before you first opened the page.

    Buttons

    WALK Walk there / Ride there Opens Google Maps navigation to the bike, on foot or by bike.
    DROP Directions to drop hub Cycling directions to whichever hub you have selected.
    NOT
    THERE     Not there Someone took it, or it is not where the feed says. Removes it and promotes the next one. Undo brings them all back.

    Why hub 1 is often not the closest hub. The list is ranked by the walk that comes after you drop, not by the ride to get there. Riding is roughly three times faster than walking, so a longer ride that leaves you beside your next bike beats a short ride that strands you.

    All distances are straight lines. Nothing here accounts for the escarpment, one-way streets, or anything you cannot cut through. Treat them as comparisons between options rather than as real travel distances.
